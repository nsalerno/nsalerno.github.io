Disclaimer and ground rules

This blog is mainly about my opinions on all things software engineering. As such, they are exactly that, my opinions. They are not irrefutable facts. My goal is to have the reader think about the topic at hand. It isn’t about changing minds. It’s all about starting a conversation, and not necessarily between you the reader and me the author as two people speaking to each other. Sometimes the conversation is within your own head and you are talking to yourself, trying to change your own mind or trying to defend your own position. That is the real goal for me. I state an opinion that starts a conversation.

And it truly is a conversation because I find myself still involved in the conversation (sometimes with co-workers, sometimes in my own head). I haven’t figured out the one true answer for each of the various questions we have about how to build software. For some questions a one true answer may not exist. While my opinion may have a veneer of certain truth it really is just a reflection of where my understanding is today, which may not be a correct and accurate picture of the problem and solution.

Thus, anything I write in these blog posts are simply personal opinions to be taken with a grain of salt. I do not claim to be correct and that my way of thinking will improve the quality of the software you build. My advice to you, the reader, is to take what I say and try it out for yourself in an isolated scratch application. See for yourself if I’m onto something or if I’m plain wrong.

Ground rules

These are the ground rules that I will hold myself to when I write.

I will use fictitious names. I will not reveal the real name of a company or person. This is to protect the innocent, the guilty, and myself. I am not a lawyer. I am not familiar with libel and slander laws. I’m not going to become a lawyer (and I guess I won’t pay one either to have it explained to me). The best thing I can do for myself and assume the worst and give no reason whatsoever for anyone to have issue with me.

I will do my best to provide references when applicable, but there will be times when I’ll say “I’ve seen this kind of code in production” but I will not provide any reference. This is due to the rule of not revealing the names of companies or people. You are just going to have to trust that I’m telling the truth. For what it’s worth, I have split opinions about this. I don’t want to be that guy who makes a claim and expects blind trust. However, it’s that important to me that I don’t reveal names.

Lastly, I will be nice (number 3 of Dalton’s rules). The world is already full of pundits who argue emotionally and resort to fear, uncertainty, and doubt. I will be nice. I will be rational. I will make a proper argument. Furthermore, I will not talk trash about other software and programming techniques. I will point out the shortcomings of things where fair and reasonable but I will not speak ill of other software or people (I do plenty of that in person and it’s an ugly habit that I have, of which I am trying to break).

With that out of the way, let’s have a conversation.
